1. Results established

For the free-boundary finite Gibbs measure proportional to
  exp(sum_e b_e (n_x dot n_y)^2) product_x dω(n_x),
with normalized uniform spherical probability, arbitrary retained nearest-neighbor square-lattice graphs, and |b_e| <= β < infinity, the observable is
  C_Q(x,y) = <(n_x dot n_y)^2 - 1/3>.
The exact distance is r = max(|x_1-y_1|, |x_2-y_2|).

At every β >= 0, there are positive constants Cβ and cβ, chosen before the graph, edge couplings, and marked sites, such that
  |C_Q(x,y)| <= Cβ exp(-cβ [log(2+r)]^2).
The endpoint is RP2.log_square_quadrupolar_decay_signed. Its statement has no assumed response, kernel-comparison, or decay estimate. It constructs the required thin-annulus comparison from the actual spin model.

For 0 <= β < log(3/2),
  |C_Q(x,y)| <= (8/9) exp(-mβ r),
  mβ = -log(max(1/2, 3(1-exp(-β)))) > 0.
The endpoint is RP2.elementary_exponential_quadrupolar_decay_signed. The underlying finite-distance bound (8/9)[3(1-exp(-β))]^r holds for every β >= 0, although it only decays in this high-temperature regime. The floor in the mass covers β=0, and the proofs also cover coincident sites.

Both theorems have signed free-boundary local-limit versions with the same constants, as well as ferromagnetic specializations. The preceding superpolynomial, moment-summability and smooth-Fourier-series conclusions remain. Adjacent annuli and two-endpoint families also supply stronger factorial bounds; the two-endpoint leading log(r)log(log(r)) coefficient is 2/log(2).

3. Focused mathematical and semantic review

The model definitions use unit vectors in R³, normalized spherical product measure, the squared interaction, the genuine normalized Gibbs expectation, and a strictly positive partition denominator. Interaction and observable are invariant under independent changes of spin sign, so this is the claimed RP² model. The retained graph definition represents each lattice bond once.

For the all-temperature improvement, the review traced plateau-rescaled profiles and their discrete cubic error; whole-block frequency truncation and its commutator normalization; the thin-shell support count; the width-independent column-energy bound; the Gram inequality retaining column energy; the actual-model fourth-order response; exact restricted pinned kernels; and integer shell packing. The resulting error is Oβ(1/(δ n)) + Oβ(δ^-6 2^-n). Choosing δ comparable to 1/n allows linearly many half-contractions in the nth dyadic band, hence a quadratic cumulative exponent and the claimed all-distance log-square bound.

For high temperature, subtracting min(b_e,0) from each edge energy gives a nonnegative activity without changing the normalized Gibbs measure. Tetrahedral averaging kills disconnected active-edge contributions. The probability of a prescribed active set S is bounded by (1-exp(-β))^|S|, and at most 4*3^(r-1) simple prefixes of length r start at a vertex. This gives the prefactor 8/9. No independence, FKG inequality, or external random-cluster criticality theorem is assumed.

The local-limit condition is convergence of actual two-spin marginals along a common sequence of free finite-volume models. It is not a predicate that assumes the desired decay. Outside fillings used to embed finite configurations add no interactions.

4. Scope and trust boundary

The log-square estimate is superpolynomial but weaker than every stretched exponential exp(-c r^α), α>0. All-temperature exponential decay is still open in this development. The stated results exclude algebraic quadrupolar tails with fixed logarithmic corrections; they do not by themselves prove absence of every thermodynamic or vortex transition.

The formalization does not prove existence or uniqueness of infinite-volume limits, a DLR characterization, decay for arbitrary interacting pinned boundaries, analyticity of free energy, or correlation bounds for arbitrary bond/vortex observables. The sharper FK thresholds in the historical analytical addendum remain unformalized and are not used in the new endpoints. The SO(3)/RP³ target-space frustrated-magnet problem is a different model.

Both replay routes use Lean's own kernel implementation, not an independently implemented proof checker. The kernel, import/runtime infrastructure, platform, and three standard axioms remain trusted. This is a fresh computational audit with focused source review, not a human referee report or a claim that a remote CI workflow ran.

For this environment only, a small supplied runtime shim maps the executable's own /proc/<pid>/exe readlink to /proc/self/exe. It changes executable-resource discovery, not proof terms or kernel checks. One worker controls memory usage. The existing theorem-local 400,000-heartbeat fix is retained; asynchronous elaboration was not disabled.
